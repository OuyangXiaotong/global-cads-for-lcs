How this catalogue is built
Compiled from regulatory databases, then matched to the literature

Products first

The catalogue is built from regulatory databases, not from the literature. Candidate products come from FDA 510(k) + PMA clearances, the FDA AI/ML-enabled device list, and CE-marked products (AIforRadiology); PubMed is then searched for those specific product names. This keeps the focus on commercially deployed software rather than academic prototypes.

Finding every product

Name-based FDA queries miss products branded without an anatomical word (e.g. AVIEW, qCT LN Quant). A reproducible manufacturer-based search recovers them — for every developer identified, its clearances under the lung-relevant product codes are retrieved regardless of device name.

What's included

Candidates are screened to CT tools whose task is lung nodule detection, characterisation, or the screening workflow. Scanner acquisition options, pulmonary-function software, general PACS, and other-organ platforms are excluded. Near-duplicate clearance variants of one product are merged.

Method classification

Each product is grouped by the method it actually uses — rule-based, classical ML/radiomics, deep learning, or multimodal/foundation — determined from the product itself, not inferred from its clearance year.

Linked papers

Each product links to papers in the search corpus that name it. These are automatic name matches; a paper may describe the product in a context other than screening, and many products have no dedicated publication yet.

Sources

openFDA 510(k) & PMA · FDA AI/ML-Enabled Devices list · AIforRadiology.com (CE) · PubMed / NCBI E-utilities. Regulatory numbers link to the FDA database; papers link to PubMed. Corrections welcome via Suggest an edit.
